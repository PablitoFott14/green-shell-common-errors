3.2The weight rewards the restatement, not the reasoning

Costs pointsIncorrect Weights

The mistake

The criteria that need the hard reasoning sit at +1, while a criterion restating their conclusions, here on the Trajectory, carries the +5.

Do this instead

Price each criterion by how hard it is to satisfy. Count the axes it exercises: source coordination, reasoning depth, modality, discovery effort. Three or more is +5, exactly two is +3, one or none is +1. A restatement never takes the weight.

How it breaks the standardThe +1s price a searched and inferred finding as a single lookup, at least a level below the Guidelines' table, and the +5 sits on the restatement the Guidelines rule out. The score then turns on the route: the three findings on the page are worth 3 points between them, the trajectory restating them 5.

The rule it breaksGuidelines · 5.2 Rubric Weights It restates a finding another criterion already scored at 5. The weight sits on the reasoning act, never on the restatement Criterion 25 is also a process criterion: it counts toward the 20% cap.

Catch it in your own task

For every +5, ask which criterion actually requires the reconciliation. For every +1, ask whether it really is a single source lookup.

Why it matters

Weight is difficulty, never importance, and the weight sits on the reasoning act, never on the restatement. When the weight sits on the restatement, the route decides the score instead of what the deliverable says. Incorrect Weights is a named Moderate issue, downgraded to Minor when the fix changes no rating, as a weight fixed on its own never does.
