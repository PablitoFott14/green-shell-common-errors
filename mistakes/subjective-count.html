4.1Fewer than ten subjective criteria

Costs points7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics

The mistake

A deliverable rich enough to judge, a deck, a transcript, interactive views, gets a subjective block of five or seven criteria, and most of the render is never graded.

Do this instead

Compare the Model A and Model B artifacts side by side as the user, and write a criterion for each visible property that improves or worsens the experience. Expect ten or more, and never pad to reach them.

Example 11Work productivity · Producing work deliverables · single turn

What is wrongFive subjective criteria for one page that carries a speaker labelled transcript and six slides: two judge the transcript, two judge single slides and one the order of the parts. Four of the six slides are never judged.

The deliverable is outputs/publix_promo_depth_0901.html: a transcript of the promo depth section and a six slide deck in a fixed structure.

Where it shows

Subjective criteria
Nothing on four of the six slides, on legibility at slide size, or on consistency across the deck.
Prompt
a clean transcript of the section where they set the promo depth, and a six-slide deck for Sun-Hee…

What it should have been

Subjective criteria
Ten or more, each one element and one visible property, for instance: every slide title in outputs/publix_promo_depth_0901.html sits in the same position; body text on every slide is legible at full screen size; speaker names in the transcript are set apart from the spoken words.

How it breaks the standardA page with a transcript and six slides gives a reader plenty to judge, and the Guidelines expect ten or more criteria from it. With five, most of what the user would compare between the two runs is never graded.

Example 12Travel · Planning trips · single turn

What is wrongSeven subjective criteria for an interactive page with ten day sections, a two state toggle and an open questions panel. Nothing judges the toggle's visible state, or overlap, clipping and legibility.

The deliverable is mum_knee_trip.html: one section per day, a toggle between two lists and a panel of open questions.

Where it shows

Subjective criteria
No criterion on the toggle's visible state, or on overlap, clipping or legibility.
Prompt
a section for each day, a toggle between the two lists, and the open questions in a panel of their own.

What it should have been

Subjective criteria
Ten or more, for instance: the toggle in mum_knee_trip.html shows which list is on with visibly different styling; every day section repeats the same order of date, items and notes; the text of every day section stays inside its section's box.

How it breaks the standardTen sections, a toggle and a panel are far more render than seven criteria can cover, and the Guidelines expect ten or more. The parts left out are exactly where two runs would look different to the user.

The rule it breaksGuidelines · 7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics The goal is to create a scenario that is complex and complete enough to naturally justify 10+ meaningful subjective criteria, rather than forcing extra rubrics just to hit the number.

Catch it in your own task

List the parts of the render: each view, section, slide and control. A part with no subjective criterion is a part nobody judged.

Where the correct guidance is written

Section titles as they print, so you can search for them in the document.

Guidelines · 7. Subjective Rubric Block

Subjective rubrics play a core role in the data the client expects us to produce, so we need to take real ownership of how they are created.
Why it matters

The Guidelines expect ten or more subjective criteria from a scenario complete enough to justify them. Subjective rubrics play a core role in the data the client expects, and an ungraded part of the render scores both models the same however differently they built it.

From the taskThe prompt, sent to Model A
It's Thursday 17 September. Mum's right knee replacement is on Tuesday 6 October at Milton Keynes University Hospital, and Imogen and I will be with her for ten days around it. The flights are booked and the trip's on my calendar, but that's about as far as I've got. She'll spend some of our stay on the ward and the rest recovering at home. I've attached what Mum sent me after her pre-op on Tuesday; everything else is spread across the last few months. She's changed her mind on a few things along the way, so where she's said two different things, go with the latest. Work out what each day needs, from the day we land to the day we fly home, and put each day's plan on my calendar on that date. Then split it into two lists, what we have to book or sort before we set off and what can be left until we've arrived, and say why each thing goes where it does. That includes Imogen's and my own arrangements, not just Mum's. Anything you can't confirm from a source, don't guess; put it with the open questions. I'd like it all in one interactive page, mum_knee_trip.html: a section for each day, a toggle between the two lists, and the open questions in a panel of their own.
