5.2Too few subjective criteria

Costs pointsRubric Criteria - Subjective Block Scope

The mistake

The subjective block holds fewer than ten criteria, so whole regions of the render go ungraded.

Do this instead

Add a criterion for each region nothing grades, from a deliverable rich enough to justify them, never padded to hit the count.

Example 21Red ShellProperty & Space · 4 Leg A turns

What is wrongThe subjective block holds five criteria, where the Guidelines expect ten or more, and none grades the cutting diagrams or the scribe plan Leg A turn 1 asks for.

Leg A turn 1 asks for front_room_builtin.html: a cut list in mm, how to scribe the edges that meet the wall and ceiling, and a cutting diagram for each sheet.

Where it shows

Subjective block
Five criteria, where the Guidelines expect ten or more.
Missing subjective criterion
Nothing grades how the cutting diagrams or the scribe plan read.

What it should have been

Subjective block
Ten or more, region by region, each naming front_room_builtin.html, the cutting diagrams and the scribe plan included.

How it breaks the standardObjective criteria 14 and 25 check the parts in the three cutting diagrams, and objective criterion 13 that the scribe plan names the part at each edge. No subjective criterion looks at how either reads, so an unreadable diagram scores the same as a clear one. It costs points.

The Green Shell rule it breaksGuidelines · 7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics The expected number of subjective rubrics is 10 or more

Catch it in your own task

Count the rows, ten at least, then check that every region of the render has one.

Where the Green Shell guidance is written

Guidelines · 7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics

The expected number of subjective rubrics is 10 or more, but do not optimize for the count itself.

Guidelines · 7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics

Focus on the render side never asked in the prompt: layout, visual hierarchy, spacing, readability, styling, structure, tone/register, consistency, and overall polish.

Guidelines · 7.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics

Weights measure impact on the user experience, not difficulty

There is no cap on the subjective block's share of the weight in Green Shell.

Why it matters

Ten is a floor, not a target. Under the count, the block cannot cover a multi panel deliverable: a nested table, a timeline strip or a set of diagrams goes ungraded, and two runs presenting the same correct figures very differently score the same. Red Shell also capped the block at 30% of the weight; Green Shell does not: a subjective weight measures the impact on the user.

From the taskLeg A prompt, turn 1, to Model A
Leg A turn 1
Hi, I need a plan for the built-in on the front room wall, the one between the corner and the door. Halvard agreed to build it, but he needs a proper cut list as the wall isn't a perfect square. I measured it and took photos at the floor, middle and ceiling levels + made a sketch, and keep in mind that the photos are the ground truth for the measurements if something's off. I have a voice memo of what plywood he has and a sticky note with his shop rules attached so stick to those. It has to fit my drafting table, my bound notebooks(standing up) + my printing plates. I included a picture of my notebooks to see their sizes from and leave space for two more seasons of notebooks(my calendar has info on how many I do per season). I still have to verify something on the photo of the plates, so skip that for now. Write all up as front_room_builtin.html - the cut list in mm with each part sized to the real wall, how to scribe the edges that meet the wall and ceiling and a cutting diagram for each sheet.
